# *Alopecurus pratensis* (meadow foxtail)

The spec sheet the bundled *Alopecurus pratensis* preset is dialled to. Each value is
a range from the sources below; where they disagree, the Flora of New Zealand
(the most detailed treatment) wins and the others are noted.

## Habit

A loosely to compactly tufted perennial grass, often with short rhizomes.
Many culms per tuft, erect or bent at the base, unbranched, each ending in a
single dense spike-like panicle. Early flowering: April to June in the UK.

| Part | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Culm height | 30-100 cm (to 110-120 cm) | NZ Flora; GrassBase; Wikipedia |
| Culm nodes | few (2-4), internodes hairless, ridged | NZ Flora |
| Leaves | mostly basal; a few on the culm | NZ Flora |
| Leaf blade | 10-30 cm long (mostly 10-20 cm), 2-5 mm wide | NZ Flora |
| Blade form | flat, finely ribbed, tapering to a fine point, margins finely rough | NZ Flora |
| Leaf sheath | green, hairless; lower sheaths dark brown, sometimes purplish | NZ Flora |
| Ligule | 1-2 mm, membranous, blunt | NZ Flora |

## The flower head

| Part | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Panicle | spike-like, cylindrical, 3.5-9.5 cm long (2-13 cm) | NZ Flora; GrassBase |
| Panicle width | about 6-8 mm | Illinois Wildflowers |
| Colour | silvery green to purplish, soft | NZ Flora; Naturescape |
| Spikelets | densely packed, solitary, 4-6 mm, one floret, falling entire | GrassBase |
| Glumes | 4-6 mm, nearly equal, keeled, fringed with fine hairs | NZ Flora |
| Awn | 4.5-8 mm from near the lemma's base, 3-4 mm past the glumes | NZ Flora |
| Anthers | 2-3.5 mm, yellow to purple | NZ Flora |

It is often confused with timothy (*Phleum pratense*). Meadow foxtail's head
is softer, and each spikelet has one soft awn where timothy's have two short,
stiff points.
